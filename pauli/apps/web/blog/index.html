MAXPAY FIELD NOTES

Follow the money.

The future of payments is not one coin replacing everything. It is dollars, deposits, stablecoins, ledgers and software becoming programmable—and more routes becoming available to people and machines.

FIELD NOTE 004

The dollar is becoming internet-native.

On July 18, 2025, the GENIUS Act became U.S. law and established a federal framework for payment stablecoins. That matters because a dollar-backed token can move on internet-native rails even when the user is nowhere near a traditional U.S. bank branch.

The useful thesis: crypto does not automatically replace the dollar. Dollar stablecoins can extend the dollar into programmable networks.

FIELD NOTE 005

The banks are tokenizing too.

BIS Project Agorá has brought together central banks and more than 40 regulated financial institutions to test tokenized commercial-bank deposits and tokenized central-bank reserves on programmable infrastructure for cross-border settlement.

The useful thesis: the serious institutional question is no longer “blockchain or banks?” It is increasingly “what kind of programmable ledger will banks use?”

FIELD NOTE 006

Ripple, XRPL, XRP and RLUSD are not the same thing.

Ripple is a company. XRPL is the XRP Ledger. XRP is the ledger's native asset. RLUSD is Ripple's dollar-backed stablecoin. Mixing those four together makes it almost impossible to reason clearly about the technology.

The useful thesis: XRPL can be infrastructure, XRP can act as a native/bridge asset, and RLUSD can provide price-stable dollar settlement. Those are separate jobs.

FIELD NOTE 007

Use the old rails where they help. Own alternatives where they matter.

For MaxPay's internal pilot, USDC is a strong first programmable rail. Cash App is also strategically useful in the U.S. because customers already understand it. A Cash App Business Account can receive payments for goods and services into a Cash App balance, but it remains a KYCed, Block-controlled payment service—not the sovereign core.

The useful thesis: MaxPay should be multi-rail: direct programmable settlement where possible, familiar payment methods where useful, one normalized ledger underneath.

FIELD NOTE 001

Why HTTP 402 matters now.

x402 turns “Payment Required” into a usable internet interaction. A resource can state its price, software can satisfy the requirement, and the server can verify payment before returning the result. The buyer can be a human, an app or an AI agent operating under a policy and budget.

Four payment ideas worth keeping.

Exact

Known price. Known amount. One verifiable result.

Up to

Authorize a maximum and settle actual usage—useful for metered services and agents.

Batch settlement

Aggregate many tiny machine interactions instead of forcing a full settlement on every request.

Session / time-limited access

Grant a defined window of access without requiring a permanent purchase or monthly subscription.

Sovereignty is redundancy.

MaxPay is not built around the claim that banks disappear. Our working definition is simpler: no single provider should be able to stop the entire business. We want a common interface across rails, owner-controlled keys where appropriate, verifiable receipts and a path around individual provider failures.
