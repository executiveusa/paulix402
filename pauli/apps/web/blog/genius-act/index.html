FIELD NOTE 004 · STABLECOINS

The dollar is becoming internet-native.

The important stablecoin story is not simply “crypto replaces dollars.” A regulated dollar token can move the dollar onto programmable networks.

What changed.

On July 18, 2025, the United States enacted the GENIUS Act, creating a federal framework for payment stablecoins. That does not turn every stablecoin into risk-free money, but it signals that payment stablecoins are moving from regulatory gray area toward formal financial infrastructure.

For MaxPay, the practical implication is straightforward: a business can increasingly treat a dollar-denominated token as one payment rail among several—alongside cards, Cash App, bank transfers and other networks.

The deeper idea.

A stablecoin such as USDC can make a U.S.-dollar-denominated asset usable by wallets and software. The customer does not need to understand correspondent banking for the payment to move across a blockchain network.

That is why the phrase “the dollar escapes the banking interface” is useful—but not literal. Stablecoins still have issuers, reserves, rules and compliance dependencies. What changes is the interface and settlement rail.

What MaxPay takes from this.

  • Price products in familiar units.
  • Let users choose an available rail.
  • Verify settlement independently.
  • Keep the internal receipt format the same regardless of rail.
  • Never confuse self-custody of a token with complete independence from the token issuer.
